# Social and mobile computing concepts applied to Folio

Every concept below is taken from the unit's own slides and readings (named in the Source column). For each one: what the concept says, where Folio uses it, where to see it, and the design tension or trade-off. The tension column is the critical part: markers reward trade-offs more than name-dropping.

| Concept | Source | What it says | Where Folio uses it | See it in | Tension / trade-off we accepted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3C collaboration model** (Fuks et al. 2008): communication, coordination, cooperation, supported by awareness | Week 2 slides | Collaboration emerges from exchanging ideas, organising people and resources, and working on shared objects, mediated by awareness | *Communication:* comments anchored to a pick; one fan question a day and a private reply. *Coordination:* one Edition per city per day (a curator claims a day); team approval of curators; midnight lock. *Cooperation:* the Edition is a shared object built by a curator from artist pitches and readers' Backs. *Awareness:* curator card, Discovery rate, countdown to midnight | Flows F2, F3, F4, F5 | More communication channels would add noise. We chose anchored comments and one question a day, not chat. |
| **Time-space matrix** (groupware) | Week 2 slides | Ask when and where people interact, and which cues are gained or lost | Mostly *different time, same city*: a reader meets the curator's Edition hours later. *Same time, same place* at a gig through Nearby. Midnight lock gives a shared daily rhythm | Today, Browse > Nearby events | Asynchronous use loses the live cues of face-to-face. We replace them with a named curator and artist replies, not live presence. |
| **Awareness and social presence** (Oh, Bailenson and Welch 2018) | Week 2 slides | Awareness helps people anticipate others and feel "real" and connected | Named curator on every Edition; Discovery check shows others found something new (as a rate, once there are enough answers); artists reply by name | Curator card; F5 | Awareness has costs (next row). |
| **Awareness tensions: visibility vs privacy; useful context vs distraction** | Week 2 slides | More visibility helps coordination but harms privacy; more context becomes noise | Fan names are visible to the artist only, and only when the fan connects. Discovery totals never show who answered. No follower counts, likes or ranking. Location is optional and never stored | F5; Settings; privacy register | We give up social proof (counts, trending) to protect privacy and keep the feed calm. This may reduce engagement; the evaluation asks. |
| **Bad awareness: Facebook Beacon** (context failure, privacy) | Week 2 slides | Auto-posting off-platform behaviour to others breaks context and privacy | Nothing is posted about what a person listens to or saves. Saves are private. Guests need no profile | Me > Saved; guest join | Slower organic growth. We accepted that. |
| **Design for Location framework**: foundation (motivations, precedents, site, people, resources) and experience (Theme/Narrative, Nodes, Trajectory, Tools, Public Face) | Frameworks slides (designforlocation.org) | Move between understanding the context and structuring the experience; do not force every part | *Theme:* one human-picked Edition per city per day. *Nodes:* Today, Liner Note, Discovery check, Browse, Nearby, Studio, Team. *Trajectory:* arrive, read, react, close; midnight lock; tomorrow. *Tools:* React, Supabase, GitHub Pages. *Public Face:* wordmark, poster, QR code | `04_Design_Process/DESIGN_PROCESS_OVERVIEW.md` | Site is a city, not a building; we treat the city as the place. |
| **Locales Framework heuristics** (Greenberg et al. 2000) | Evaluation slides | Evaluate groupwork, not just task work: provide locales, awareness within locales, individual views, evolving interactions over time, relating locales | *Locales:* the city Edition page gathers people (curator, artists, readers), artefacts (picks) and a shared purpose. *Individual views:* role-based landing (member, curator, team). *Over time:* archive and curator standing tiers. *Relating locales:* city picker | `05_Research_Evaluation/EVALUATION_KIT.md` | Heuristics found problems in a one-city app; relating locales is only lightly tested because only Brisbane is live. |
| **Groupware walkthrough** (Pinelle and Gutwin 2002) | Evaluation slides | Walk realistic collaborative scenarios to find collaboration-specific problems without full user tests | The five flows are written as walkthrough scenarios (`02_Product/USER_FLOWS_AND_TEST_RESULTS.md`) | F1 to F5 | A walkthrough by the team is not a substitute for users; we label it as such. |
| **Mobile and contextual possibilities** (where, when, what else is around) | Frameworks slides | Match people's issues to what mobile and context make possible; ask what happens when people shift locations | Optional "use my location" picks the nearest live city; Nearby events by city; one-handed layout; short bounded session | Settings > City; Browse | Location creep. We keep it opt-in, in the browser only, never stored. |
| **Winner (1980) and Akrich (1992)**: artefacts have politics and scripts | Week 9 annotated tasks | Design decisions embed values and prescribe behaviour | The no-like, no-ranking rules and the named curator are deliberate politics. The curator role is applied for, not chosen. We write the script (read, react, close) openly | `06_Ethics/ETHICAL_CONSIDERATIONS.md` | A small group of curators shapes taste. Backs and Discovery results, and team approval, are the checks. |
| **Prototyping: build only what serves the goals; do not let the prototype become the product** | Prototyping slides | Quick, throw-away prototypes first; scope to the question | Base44 was a throw-away look-and-feel prototype; the repo build is a proof of concept with a stated "not a product" list | `04_Design_Process/` | Real login goes beyond the brief; justified in the MVP spec. |
