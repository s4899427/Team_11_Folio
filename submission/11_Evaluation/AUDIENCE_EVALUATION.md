# Audience Research and Week 12 Tradeshow Review

## Audience Research

### Intended Audience

We designed Folio for people who want a more personal way to discover local music, particularly newcomers who are unfamiliar with a city’s artists, venues and music communities. Our initial focus was Brisbane, with international students new to Australia providing a clear use case.

We also considered listeners who feel dissatisfied with algorithmic feeds, emerging artists seeking visibility and curators who want to share their knowledge. These groups have different needs: listeners need relevant and trustworthy recommendations, artists need fair representation, and curators need a clear process for contributing.

### Early Research and Concept Development

Our initial concept, MOTIF, explored a broad platform for discovering world culture. Across six first-round interviews, a recurring concern was that algorithmic discovery had made recommendations feel repetitive and impersonal. This encouraged us to explore human curation as an alternative.

A second round of five interviews highlighted a distinction between meaningful inspiration and simply passing time. Interest in curator-led discovery also depended on whether participants considered the curator credible. One interview was a team member’s self-interview, which we treated as weaker evidence.

These findings, alongside peer and tutor feedback, contributed to narrowing the concept. We moved from a broad cultural platform towards local music and developed Folio’s daily Edition format, named curators and sourced Liner Notes. The final choice of 8–12 picks was a team judgement rather than a directly established audience preference.

### Formative User Evaluation

Before the tradeshow, we conducted two short evaluation sessions with one participant in each session. We used observation and interview questions to explore how participants understood the app and where they experienced difficulty.

One participant interpreted Browse primarily as an events guide. The other, who was new to Brisbane, valued the local information. These responses suggested that Folio’s contextual content had potential value, but the relationship between music discovery, events and discussion needed to be clearer.

Neither participant successfully accessed commenting. Both were anonymous readers, and the participation prompt appeared near the bottom of a long page. We responded by adding a Discussion shortcut, a clearer “Join as a guest” prompt and larger tap targets.

These sessions provided useful formative feedback, but their small size and the participants’ existing connections to the team limit the conclusions we can draw. They did not test simultaneous multi-user interaction or demonstrate sustained use over several daily Editions.

## Week 12 Tradeshow Review

### Overall Reception

The peer assessments showed that reviewers generally understood Folio’s central proposition: a daily, city-specific music experience curated by people rather than recommendation algorithms. Several reviewers valued the combination of limited selections, named curators and contextual information.

Reviewers described the experience as more personal and less overwhelming than an endless feed. They also recognised its potential to give smaller local artists greater visibility. This supports the clarity of our concept, although peer feedback alone does not establish whether people would use Folio regularly.

### Social and Contextual Experience

Peers recognised that Folio connects music discovery to place. Local artists, Liner Notes and nearby gigs helped communicate a relationship between listening and Brisbane’s cultural context.

However, several reviewers wanted more visible opportunities to participate, such as sharing thoughts, recommending songs or contributing to discussions. One reviewer felt that the prototype resembled a music information page more than an interactive social app.

Because commenting already existed, we interpreted this feedback partly as a discoverability and demonstration problem. We needed to show the social experience more clearly and make the transition from reading to participating easier to understand.

Some suggestions, such as adding reactions or personalised recommendations, would require careful consideration. We would assess whether they support useful interaction while preserving our intention to avoid popularity-driven discovery.

### Pitch and Poster

Feedback on the pitch and poster was generally positive. Reviewers found the daily, city-based concept clear and described the poster as organised and visually consistent with the music magazine idea.

One reviewer noted that smaller text and screenshots were difficult to read from a distance. This highlighted the need to design specifically for the tradeshow environment, where visitors may only spend a short time looking at a display.

For a future presentation, we would reduce text density, enlarge key interface examples and organise the demonstration around a simple user journey. This would help visitors understand both the concept and what they can actually do in the app.

### Functionality and Simulation

Responses to the prototype’s functionality were mixed. Some reviewers recognised a working core involving guest access, Editions, source links and curator workflows. Others were uncertain which features were functional and which were simulated.

This difference suggests that our demonstration did not communicate implementation boundaries consistently. Reviewers recommended clearer labels for simulated features and a short end-to-end walkthrough showing discovery, participation and curator contribution.

The midnight lock was also difficult to demonstrate during a brief visit. A clearly labelled demonstration control could make this behaviour visible without asking visitors to wait for a daily transition.

We should also explain that following external links is part of the discovery experience while keeping enough useful information within Folio for users to understand what they are opening.

### Local Discovery and Map Integration

Several assessments suggested strengthening the connection between recommendations and nearby music experiences. Suggestions included a map of gigs, clearer venue information and easier access to dates, locations and ticket links.

This feedback identified a gap between discovering an artist and acting on that interest. An interactive map could connect featured artists with relevant venues and events, helping users explore the local scene beyond the daily Edition.

We would retain optional location access and provide manual suburb search and a list view. Venue and event details would also need regular checking so the map does not create false confidence in outdated information.

### Trust, Fairness and Curator Selection

Peers recognised named curators and sourced explanations as valuable features. However, they also questioned whether human curation could favour particular genres, social circles or established artists.

One challenging scenario described a curator repeatedly selecting artists from the same network, excluding other parts of Brisbane’s music community. Reviewers therefore recommended clearer explanations of curator selection and how different artists receive opportunities to appear.

This reinforced that human curation makes responsibility visible but does not remove bias. We would strengthen curator approval criteria, conflict-of-interest disclosures and opportunities to question inaccurate attribution or unfair representation.

### Daily Editions and Continued Discovery

One reviewer raised a scenario in which a user discovers an artist shortly before midnight but cannot return to the recommendation after the Edition changes. This highlighted a tension between a finite daily experience and the practical need to revisit useful information.

A future iteration could explore saved picks or read-only past Editions while retaining discussion on the current Edition. We would test whether this supports continued discovery without weakening the simplicity of the daily format.

### Communicating Research More Clearly

Reviewers noticed that interviews and feedback had influenced the move from MOTIF to Folio. However, several wanted clearer evidence connecting specific findings to specific design decisions.

We would improve this by showing a concise research-to-design table, selected participant quotations and before-and-after examples. We would distinguish interview findings, tutor feedback, usability observations and team decisions so that our process remains traceable without overstating the evidence.

## Priorities Following the Tradeshow

Based on the assessments, our next priorities would be to:

1. **Clarify the user journey:** Make participation easier to find and demonstrate the complete experience.
2. **Explain prototype boundaries:** Clearly identify working, simulated and planned features.
3. **Strengthen local discovery:** Connect artists, venues and events through clearer information and map integration.
4. **Build trust and safety:** Develop moderation, correction processes and transparent curator criteria.
5. **Support continued discovery:** Test ways to revisit useful picks after the daily Edition changes.
6. **Run a longer pilot:** Evaluate several Editions with local listeners, newcomers, artists and rotating curators.

The tradeshow feedback helped us identify where Folio’s concept was convincing and where its interaction design remained unclear. Our next evaluation should test whether users can move confidently from reading an Edition to discussing music, revisiting discoveries and exploring the local scene.
