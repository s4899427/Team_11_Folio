# The Pivot: MOTIF → Folio

## Before — MOTIF
A wiki for world culture: music, fashion, art, and food, browsable at will.
Wide in scope, but undefined in what made it different from Wikipedia,
Spotify, or Instagram doing the same things separately. No clear domain
statement. No answer to who curates or what stops it becoming another
algorithmic feed.

## The evidence that forced the decision
- Six independent interviews (Marushka, Pritesh, Minahil, Rayyan, Sully,
  Aloysious) converged unprompted on the same complaint: algorithmic
  discovery has "ruined the taste," and people want something more
  human-curated.
- Peer feedback (Team 8) asked the question we didn't yet have an answer
  to: who decides accuracy when sources disagree?
- Course feedback identified the strongest part of the concept as the idea
  of one curated daily experience — not a browsable library.

## After — Folio, "Today's Thread"
One curated cultural experience a day, assembled from any number of
elements from any domain (not fixed to music/art/fashion), picked by a
rotating verified curator, and locking permanently at midnight. No
algorithm anywhere. Every post ties to a specific entry and passes an
automated relevance/safety check. Interpretive content shows multiple
attributed perspectives rather than one enforced "truth." A lightweight
coordination feature lets people see what a few others are wearing or
listening to for an occasion — without a follower graph.

## Why this counts as one pivot, not several
Every individual change along the way — dropping full music streaming,
generalising the Thread beyond three fixed categories, replacing
"who's right" with "whose perspective is this," adding the friends'-theme
feature — was a direct consequence of the same underlying decision: stop
building a browsable library, and build one bounded, evidenced, human-
curated daily experience instead. The destination was reached once,
deliberately, from a clear body of evidence — not arrived at by drift.
