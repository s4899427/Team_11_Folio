# ROUND 1 — Concept-stage interviews

## R1-P1 (Ex-UQ graduate)
**Q: Typical week — where does music/style fit in?**
Music fills every gap — involuntary while commuting/traveling, voluntary at home for vibe/exploration. Style is functional but wants to keep it interesting, not just basics.

**Q: Where does your style/taste come from?**
Family — an aunt's Y2K style as a fashion role model; different uncles for different music genres (old rock, old Hindi); dad for classics (Elvis, Michael Jackson).

**Q: Found something through a friend, not an algorithm?**
"My playlist is basically just a large puzzle with pieces from different people I've met across my entire life."

**Q: Frustrating about current discovery?**
Sometimes can't put outfit ideas together even with pieces in front of her; doesn't use Pinterest much beforehand but recreates saved inspiration later.

**Q: Outfit + playlist planning?**
Yes for occasions (theme parties) — looks up inspo, matches wardrobe. Different playlists for different moods/tasks (confidence-boosting Y2K for getting ready, energetic for commute, soft/soulful for winding down).

**Q: Check friends before deciding your look?**
Has one close friend with similar-but-evolving taste; they discuss outfits before going out.

**Q: Want to know the culture/history behind what you wear?**
Yes, especially functional/fabric reasoning (breathability, weather-appropriateness).

**Q: Gut reaction to combined music+fashion+art+culture app?**
Very positive — was literally just suggesting to friends they make a shared playlist for exactly this reason (new music with familiarity). Wants culture/art added too, admits limited art education but interested.

**Q: What would you do day-to-day?**
Outfit inspo (quick, image-based, not video-heavy), browsing own saved pieces, mood-based playlists, discovering nearby events (concerts, poetry readings, art sessions) she currently misses due to poor visibility/algorithm blindness.

**Q: Concerts/events awareness?**
Currently relies on incidental YouTube suggestions; wants more proactive, earlier notice (cheaper tickets).

**Q: Awareness of local (Australian/First Nations) culture?**
Admits low awareness despite public efforts (bus posters); would like the app to help here.

**Q: What would you fix about current social media?**
Wants pre-video-era Instagram back — raw, unedited, low-effort authentic posts instead of high-production content.

## R1-P2 (Ex-UQ, industry)
**Q: Typical week — music/style?**
~2 hour daily commute is prime music time. Clothing ranges formal to casual depending on WFH/office.

**Q: Where does style/taste come from?**
Movies/TV, street style observation, followed brands (Zara, Tommy Hilfiger). Music taste from childhood, shaped by social circle and environment (Brisbane → upbeat music for motivation).

**Q: Found something through a friend, not algorithm?**
A friend introduced Linkin Park's "Bleed It Out" pre-algorithm era; also finding music from what's playing in venues.

**Q: Frustrating about discovery?**
Misses a specific era of great Spotify playlists (2017) and can't recreate that; wants better ways to express taste so recommendations actually fit the vibe.

**Q: Outfit + playlist for occasions?**
Yes situationally (e.g. hip-hop nights = dressing in black) but not a common habit.

**Q: Check friends' style before deciding?**
To a degree — assumes shared taste with friends but doesn't strictly coordinate.

**Q: Culture/history behind what you wear?**
Doesn't currently know it but would like to.

**Q: Music's effect on mood?**
Significant — upbeat music shifts outlook positively, gloomy music can reinforce a low mood (acknowledges it could be circular).

**Q: Gut reaction to the concept?**
Excited by the overlaps between music/fashion/art/culture; no major concerns stated upfront.

**Q: What would you do day-to-day?**
Input a venue/vibe, get outfit + music recommendations tailored to that specific occasion (e.g. "pre-gaming" playlist suggestions).

**Q: Concerts/events awareness?**
Important; currently uses Apple Music's built-in concert feature (switched from Shazam) since it's in the same app as his music.

**Q: Local vs. broad culture awareness?**
~70/30 split favouring local — more relatable and attendable, but still wants broader awareness.

**Q: What would you fix?**
Wants curated artists cutting through noise, less "rubbish," more experience-based discovery.

# ROUND 2 — Behaviour/motivation interviews (concept revealed only at the end)

## R2-P1 (India)
Shares visual content on Instagram, written thoughts on Twitter/Reddit. Perfectionism around sharing eased over years — platform "become more for me than for anyone else." Doesn't hold back sharing much — uses close-friends sharing features. Uses Instagram as primary cultural discovery tool, values global reach. Deliberately seeks diverse perspectives rather than one personalised feed. Names the algorithm trap directly: *"The algo circle... traps you in... you won't even know, you've blinked, an hour's gone, and you've forgotten why you came there in the first place."* On someone copying his work: depends entirely on the copier's intention — good faith is fine, bad faith should be confronted. On friend coordination: adapts to occasion formality but otherwise brings his own vibe.

## R2-P2 (India)
After creating something, first instinct is to eat, then put on music and sing along — opens Spotify, not a camera app. Keeps mood-based playlists (sad/happy/spiritual). Holds back sharing due to perfectionism — reviews content 15-20 times. Loves cooking (full creative expression) and singing along to music. Seeks cultural motivation through a mix of video, music, and speaking thoughts aloud. Used spiritual music specifically to de-stress after a hard week. Clear distinction: inspiration = intellectual value; killing time = none. On others' posts: wants to build on/improve what inspires him, moves past "brain rot." Holds back responding sometimes out of respect for others' perspective — doesn't want to "poke my nose" into their way of thinking. Adapts to group preference before going out but will still suggest his own idea. On someone copying his work: feels flattered, doesn't need credit — "if they're getting inspired, that means I'm doing something good."

## R2-P3 (University student)
Shares to Instagram story/group chat first; main account only if genuinely proud. Checks how something looks as a story, or asks a friend's opinion before posting. Holds back if too personal, unfinished, or fears comparison to others' content. Moves across Instagram/TikTok/Spotify by category. Real example: outfit search for dinner — cross-platform browsing (Instagram → TikTok), saved ideas, showed friends before deciding. Clear inspiration-vs-killing-time distinction: active saving/searching vs. scrolling with nothing remembered. Mostly lurks on others' posts; if inspired, saves privately rather than publicly building on it. Held back replying to avoid seeming competitive or "making it about them." Reaction to someone's similar take: flattering if it's clearly their own version; annoyed only at an exact, unacknowledged copy. Confirms real friend coordination for birthdays/dinners — group chat asks what everyone's wearing, songs shared while getting ready, loose vibe without being a strict uniform. Willing to adjust "a little" to not feel out of place.
**On the concept**: *"It makes more sense now. I like that it's one curated thing rather than an endless feed. I could see myself checking it daily if it was quick and the Threads were actually different each day."* Category preference: music first (daily habit), then fashion.

## R2-P4 (Retail worker)
Doesn't share everything — visual/food/design ideas often sent directly to a few people rather than posted. Reviews on camera roll before deciding whether to send anywhere via WhatsApp/Instagram. Holds back often — dislikes "posting for the sake of posting." **Explicitly does not browse by open category** — follows a trail from something already trusted (an artist, a friend's recommendation, a restaurant page, a film review, an event page): *"I don't normally open an app and think 'show me culture.'"* Real trail example: a friend's song → the artist → similar artists → live performances — described this as feeling more intentional than accepting a playlist. Clear inspiration-vs-time distinction: leaves inspiration sessions with something concrete (a song, a place, an idea); killing time is automatic and unremembered. Prefers to appreciate others' work quietly rather than publicly critique unless invited. Holds back commenting due to tone concerns — online comments can read harsher than intended, doesn't want unsolicited discussion with near-strangers. On someone's similar take: mostly flattering, curious about the differences, could make an idea "feel more social rather than everyone creating in isolation." Confirms coordination for bigger events only (concert, wedding, big night out) — not everyday. Would adjust only if it affected the setting; for music, would add to the vibe rather than copy exactly.
**On the concept**: *"The daily Thread is the part I'd use. The fact that it locks at midnight gives it a reason to check without needing to scroll forever. I'd probably ignore it if the curation started feeling random or promotional."* Category preference: food or music — food connects to "places, traditions and things I can actually try."

## R2-P5 (Health professional)
Saves creative work first, decides where it belongs later — comfortable leaving things unfinished in private files. Compares mood/presentation across Pinterest/Instagram before deciding how to show something. Overthinks originality and whether posting makes something "feel less personal" — much more comfortable sharing with a small group. Uses distinct, deliberate tools per category (Pinterest for visual references, Spotify for music, Letterboxd for film, Instagram for artists/fashion); friends are a major discovery source for things she wouldn't search herself. Real example: poster references — one saved Pinterest image → related images → designer lookup → a folder of colours/type/images. Clear inspiration-vs-time distinction: inspiration builds connections between things; killing time blurs everything together — "consuming more than thinking." Wants to understand others' creative approach and sometimes make her own response, but won't critique uninvited. Avoids public responses because it can feel like "putting yourself next to their work for comparison" — sometimes creates privately and never shows the original poster. On someone's similar take: welcomes genuinely different interpretations; only annoyed by exact-execution copying. Confirms real friend coordination — outfit photos before events, shared playlists for road trips/parties/study sessions, theme emerging naturally from the group. Would treat a shared theme as "a creative prompt... while still feeling like me."
**On the concept**: *"This actually makes the concept more interesting to me. A single curated Thread feels more intentional than another feed, and the archive would be useful... I'd check it regularly if the curator choices felt credible."* Category preference: art/design first, then music — *"I'd be most interested in seeing combinations I wouldn't have put together myself."*

## R2-P6 (via Dylan's interview)
Keeps creative work (quick edits, layouts) private or shared only with close friends/family who "get the context" — rarely goes to a public feed. Reviews his own work one last time (in the gallery/editing app) before deciding to send it anywhere. Holds back due to fear of judgment and perfectionism — half-baked ideas stay private rather than risk overthinking a caption. Music is always his starting point for cultural motivation — mood-dependent genre mixing (hip-hop, EDM, old Hindi classics, local trending artists) via YouTube Music. Real example: searching for living-room layout references and sketching options to make a cramped room feel open — a design/architecture-adjacent discovery behaviour. Clear inspiration-vs-time distinction: mindless scrolling through shorts vs. actively screenshotting/comparing options while solving a real problem. Mostly moves past others' posts without commenting; rarely feels the urge to critique. Held back responding mainly due to lack of familiarity with the poster — commenting on an acquaintance's post feels "awkward," unlike someone he's actually in touch with. On someone's similar take: **a mix of emotions** — flattery, curiosity, and "a tiny bit of competitive drive." Coordinates with friends only for theme parties (group chat asking what everyone's wearing); otherwise not a planned uniform. Would roughly match a laid-back group's energy without overthinking exact details.
**On the concept**: *"This sounds really interesting... it puts all the endless [scroll] and algorithm noise [aside]... especially since it locks at midnight."* But flags adoption risk specifically: *"I'd have to see if it is generally cool or just like one more app notification I'm ignoring by Tuesday afternoon."* Category preference: music first (specifically beats over lyrics, mood-dependent), then design/film.
